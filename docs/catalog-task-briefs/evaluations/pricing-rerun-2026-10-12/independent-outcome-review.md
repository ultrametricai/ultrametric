# Pricing rerun output review

Both native arms completed useful local pricing packages. Their baseline arithmetic, customer treatment and commitment handling are correct for the supplied fictional records. UM adds invoice schedules, explicit held-discount and customer-loss scenarios, and an execution ledger. The control is easier to revise through its notice-date command-line argument. The UM model has a concrete reuse defect when that assumption changes. This case supports a bounded comparison of artifact features and weaknesses; it does not establish an overall quality lift.

## Verified baseline and useful differences

Copy-only replay reproduced all three control data outputs and all eight UM data outputs byte for byte. Both preserve all six subscription identities, hold the past-due subscription, honor the pending cancellation, retain the 20% recurring discount and fixed $99 commitment, and preserve the annual term. All four active accounts remain conditional on actual notice and approval. No provider, invoice, checkout, communication or payment operation was performed.

Both calculate retained active monthly run rate of $484.70 or $504.50, depending on whether Canal's listed $99 is before or after its discount. Conditional migration reaches $610.37, an increase of $125.67 or $105.87. With hypothetical October 12 notice, the first dates are Elm December 3, Canal December 10, Maple February 5 and Willow March 1. Neither supplies a false actual notice receipt. The scheduled cancellation is distinguished from new price-change churn; past-due nominal value is distinguished from collections.

The control delivers a complete two-interpretation ledger, month-end forecast through April, portfolio bridge including the scheduled cancellation, draft notices, runbook and verification record. It carries the unresolved Canal amount into customer copy. Its notice-date option reproduces the boundary effects: one day later can move Elm or Maple one month, or Willow one year. Its proposed provider acceptance tests remain explicitly unperformed.

UM carries both amount interpretations through four scenario schedules: unchanged prices, conditional migration, unresolved Canal hold and Elm cancellation. It separates monthly run rate from scheduled invoice subtotals and unknown cash collection. November–March simulated invoice uplift is $776 in the before-discount scenario; the large March amount includes Willow's annual charge. Holding Canal yields $586.37 or $606.17 March run rate; losing Elm yields $481.37. These are algebraic scenarios without customer-behavior probabilities. Its runbook and execution ledger make held, unchanged, failed and unconfirmed states explicit and require reconciliation before uncertain retries. The baseline package is useful for a founder's decision and billing-owner handoff.

## Material reuse limitation

In [UM model.py](arms/with-um/pricing-rollout/model.py.txt), line 19 invites a dated assumption edit, but lines 263–264 assert fixed baseline dates. In a disposable copy, changing only `ASSUMED_NOTICE` to January 31, 2027 makes the script exit with `AssertionError` after rewriting seven CSVs. The original October 12 `model-summary.json` remains in place because it is written after the assertions. A consumer ignoring the nonzero exit can now read mixed versions.

The changed ledger and date function correctly defer Willow to March 1, 2028, and the March monthly scenario correctly becomes $568.70. However, line 229 still labels the eventual $610.37 cohort contribution as March. Several explanatory notes also retain baseline dates. These defects concern reuse after changing an input, not the reproduced frozen baseline. A minimal artifact repair would separate fixed regression expectations from runtime invariants, derive horizon-specific cohort values from the schedule, and validate a full output set before replacing files. Static notes and narratives need revision with the changed assumptions. No artifact was repaired in this review.

Both packages encode policy constants rather than parsing revised commitment/proposal prose, and both require manual narrative refresh. Control's forecast ends in April; UM's monthly forecast ends in March. Later notices can defer increases beyond those horizons. Control summarizes its assertion checks in prose without saving an executable assertion suite; UM saves assertions, but couples them to this exact fixture. These limits prevent treating either package as a general billing migration tool.

## Process and evidence limits

UM naturally listed processes, read empty-history company context and opened `roll-out-a-pricing-change`, preview version 3. The native returned instructions hash matches candidate `110ff2631ac0adf37f5325c123391af643a4a7c5`. Two identical `save_update` attempts were rejected by the native approval policy; both the final answer and local status document disclose the failure. No saved-progress or cross-agent memory benefit was established. The failure identifies this runtime policy boundary, not a confirmed product persistence defect.

Control used 252,064 gross tokens (35,065 uncached input); UM used 433,320 (40,294 uncached input). Both used the same model, effort, source packet and prospective limits. Gross tokens include repeated cached context and do not establish monetary cost. UM used more time and produced more material for the operator to review.

This is one author-visible synthetic case, control first, with source-bounded answers to actual questions. A separate reviewer inspected outputs and ran deterministic copy-only checks; this was not a blind trial or an additional performer run. No real billing account, operator execution, notice delivery, retained customer result or provider compatibility was validated. The observed reuse defect warrants an artifact-level repair; this review recommends no shared guide edit from this case alone.
