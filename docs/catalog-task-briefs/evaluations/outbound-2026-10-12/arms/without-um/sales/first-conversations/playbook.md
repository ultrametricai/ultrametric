# Fieldnote Work: first conversations

Prepared 2026-10-12. Aim for two relevant learning conversations as the first milestone, with a maximum of four interviews per week. This is a working target, not a forecast.

The company record and existing contacts are synthetic. This kit is a local research and drafting exercise: no messages were sent or queued, no forms were submitted, and no meetings were booked. Public organizations below are research candidates, not customers, endorsements, or confirmed buyers. The user could not provide information about personal introductions, so this plan assumes no warm route is available.

**Start here**

1. Read [the three personalized drafts](outreach-drafts.md). Ellsworth is the first candidate to investigate: its website lists a 20-building portfolio, within the intended range. Independence, staff workflow, and interest still need qualification. [Ellsworth company page](https://ellsworthproperties.com/)
2. Use [prospects.csv](prospects.csv) for the evidence, public business contact routes, unknowns, and conversation tracking. Podium and Moore need portfolio-size qualification. There is no inferred building count from units or from a subset of a firm's portfolio.
3. Keep [local-contact-review.csv](local-contact-review.csv) separate. Those four records are fictional and cannot supply real conversations. The original contacts file is unchanged.

**The offer**

“I'm building Fieldnote Work, an early prototype that turns staff maintenance notes entered through a form into a work-order draft for a manager to review. I'd like to understand how you handle that step today.”

Ask for 15 minutes about a recent maintenance task. The prototype currently accepts form entries; email import is unfinished. There are no paying customers, endorsements, or measured results in the source materials. Do not add savings claims, imply automatic dispatch, promise integrations, or describe email import as available. A resident request form on a prospect's site does not establish that the firm has a problem with staff notes.

**Who to learn from**

The initial audience is a manager or owner at an independent US or Canadian firm responsible for 5–30 buildings. Confirm buildings, not units. The strongest workflow fit would be a manager who already turns staff observations into work orders and can describe a recent instance of missing details, rewriting, or repeated clarification. Those problems are hypotheses until the manager reports them.

A firm that already has a portal can still be informative: ask whether inspection and staff notes go straight into it. If the workflow is already satisfactory, record that finding. If the firm needs email import to try anything, record the dependency rather than promise a release date. A firm outside the size range may provide context, but should not count as evidence for the initial audience.

**The 15-minute conversation**

| Time | Ask | Capture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 min | “How many buildings does your firm manage, and who reviews staff maintenance notes?” | Portfolio size, role, independence, and who would approve a tool. |
| 2–7 min | “Walk me through the last maintenance issue a staff member reported. Where did the note arrive, and what happened before it became a work order?” | Actual steps, people, tools, and any clarifications. Ask what worked well too. |
| 7–10 min | “What, if anything, needed rewriting? How often does that happen? What did you do last time?” | Frequency and effort as reported; distinguish estimates from measured facts. Do not supply an answer for them. |
| 10–12 min | Explain the current form-to-draft prototype. If a working demo has been checked, ask whether they want to see a fictional example. “Where would this help, and where would it add work?” | Current alternative, form acceptance, missing capabilities, and reasons to decline. |
| 12–15 min | If a relevant problem exists: “Would it be useful to review one anonymized example together in a second conversation? Who else would need to be involved?” | A specific next step, the decision maker, and what would justify considering a paid tool. Otherwise thank them and record the mismatch. |

For a second conversation, agree on one example, the person who reviews the draft, and what a useful result would look like before discussing any trial. Do not promise a production rollout, pricing, or a delivery date that has not been established. Use fictional or anonymized examples without tenant identifiers.

**Record and decide**

In the tracker, leave activity dates blank until the event occurs. Move a record from `research_only` to the actual state only when supported: `contacted`, `replied`, `booked`, `completed`, `closed`, `opted_out`, or `delivery_unknown`. A sent message alone does not establish delivery. An opt-out stops contact; unknown delivery stops retries until reconciled against real message evidence.

For each completed interview, record: building count; staff-note workflow; recent example; reported frequency/effort; existing software; decision maker; form-versus-email requirement; and the agreed next action. Count completed interviews with the intended audience separately from general conversations and bookings. Positive comments alone do not demonstrate buying intent.

After the first four interviews, choose a direction from the evidence: narrow to the recurring workflow if several managers describe it; investigate another workflow if it is already solved; or prioritize email import only if managers identify it as a concrete obstacle. A willingness to share an example and involve the person who can approve a purchase is stronger evidence than praise. Four interviews remain a small exploratory sample.
