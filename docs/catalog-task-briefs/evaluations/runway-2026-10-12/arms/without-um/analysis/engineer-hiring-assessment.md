Rivet Desk — engineer hiring assessment

Prepared October 12, 2026. All amounts are USD. This analysis uses the supplied synthetic company records, with September 30 opening cash and an October 2026–March 2027 forecast. Updated actuals and customer-retention information are unavailable.

**Recommendation: defer the proposed November 1 hire.** It would leave $50,800 available at March 31, which is $24,200 below management’s $75,000 minimum buffer. The business can pay the modeled bills through March in the base case, but the hire fails the stated buffer requirement. Customer concentration makes the decision more fragile.

The customer-loss scenario assumes receipts fall 25% from November onward, from $62,000 to $46,500 per month, with no replacement receipts or spending reduction. The source says “about one quarter” and does not specify the final payment date, so this is a stress assumption, not a prediction. If the loss instead starts in December, the November-hire scenario ends March at negative $11,200 and breaches the buffer in January. Negative balances represent an unmet funding need; no overdraft is available.

**Cash and spending basis**

- September 30 bank balances total $240,800: operating $170,800 plus reserve $70,000. Subtract $25,000 of refundable customer deposits to obtain $215,800 available for operations. The remaining $45,000 in the reserve account is included as available under the supplied notes.
- Monthly existing outflows and tax set-asides total $74,500: payroll $57,000, rent $3,000, hosting $7,000, subscriptions $2,500, marketing $2,000, and tax reserve $3,000. The tax reserve is treated as unavailable for discretionary spending even if it remains in a bank account.
- Forecast receipts are $62,000 per month. This is an uncontracted planning assumption, leaving $12,500 monthly net burn before hiring and the December invoice.
- December includes an additional $12,000 annual insurance payment.
- The hire adds $14,000 per month from the start month and an $8,000 recruiter payment in October if approved. A November start therefore adds $78,000 through March: five months of compensation plus the fee. Ongoing monthly burn becomes $26,500 at base receipts.
- All approved-hire scenarios retain the October recruiter payment, including delayed starts. No-hire scenarios have no fee. A January start assumes the engineer and quoted cost remain available then; no offer has been made.

**Conditions that could change the decision**

- To start in November and just preserve the buffer under base receipts, secure $24,200 of additional cash or equivalent spending reductions during the forecast. One equivalent is $4,840 per month of extra receipts or savings for November–March. These are minimums with no remaining March headroom; timing must also be checked.
- Alternatively, the maximum fully loaded monthly hiring cost is $9,160 for a November start with the same $8,000 fee: ($128,800 − $75,000 − $8,000) ÷ 5. That is $4,840 below the proposal.
- January is the earliest modeled start month at the proposed cost that preserves the base-case buffer through March. Its $3,800 margin is too thin to rely on while customer retention and updated actuals are unknown. Deferral is an option to revisit, not a recommendation to commit now.
- If the large customer leaves from November, even no hire needs $23,700 of cash improvement by March to preserve the buffer. Approving the November hire in that scenario would require $101,700 of improvement to preserve it.

Before committing, obtain updated bank and collection actuals, confirm that the concentrated customer will remain or establish replacement receipts, and update this forecast. No future financing is assumed because none is committed. A hire continues to cost money after March; passing this six-month test alone would not establish longer-term affordability.
